Floods start in many ways: heavy rain, storm surge, a river over its banks, a dead sump pump, a sewer backup or a major plumbing failure, and all of them call for fast professional help. In nearly every case the water falls into Category 2, known as gray water, or Category 3, known as black water, which means contaminants, bacteria and other hazards that are a real health risk. Day or night, 24/7, St. George Restoration sends flood cleanup crews throughout St. George to remove floodwater, sanitize the affected rooms and safely restore your home or building.

We start with fast water extraction, using truck-mounted and submersible pumps that can move thousands of gallons per hour. Next our technicians, who hold IICRC certification, judge the level of contamination and choose a sanitizing plan that covers HEPA air filtration, antimicrobial application and disinfecting each surface that got wet. Anything unsalvageable comes out and is hauled off correctly, including wet insulation, drywall that took in contaminated water and waterlogged carpet padding.

After extraction and sanitizing, we set up commercial drying equipment, including LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers, to dry the structure of your property thoroughly. We check moisture levels every day with calibrated meters and thermal imaging so the drying is complete and mold does not get a start. Your insurance adjuster will want scope-of-work reports, equipment logs, moisture maps and photographs, so we keep that documentation going through the whole project.

How to Tell You Need Flood Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Storm damage that lets rain in through the roof or walls
  • A sump pump that failed during heavy rain and let the basement flood
  • A layer of silt, dirt or debris on the floor after water recedes
  • Soaked landscaping that leaves water pooling against your foundation
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or drywall that feels soft and wet
  • Dirty water rising out of floor drains or sewer lines into rooms you use
  • Water pooled on any floor of the building, no matter where it came from

How to Prevent Flood Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Keep gutters and storm drains clear of debris so water can flow properly
  • Install a battery backup sump pump so you stay protected when storms knock out power
  • Cracks in the foundation should be sealed and the basement walls given a waterproof coating
  • Have a backwater valve fitted to the sewer line so sewage cannot flow back inside
  • Use waterproof bins and raised shelving for anything valuable kept at ground level or below
  • In areas of the property that tend to flood, raise electrical systems and appliances off the floor

The Benefits of Professional Flood Cleanup

There is much more to a flood cleanup than removing the water you can see. Floodwater carries bacteria, sewage, chemicals and other contaminants that are serious health hazards. Professional flood crews are trained in containment, personal protective equipment (PPE) and sanitizing protocols that keep your family from exposure to pathogens such as E. coli, hepatitis and other waterborne diseases.

Health is not the only issue: when cleanup is done wrong, moisture stays hidden and heavy mold can follow in a matter of days. Professional technicians use moisture meters, thermal imaging and psychrometric calculations to make sure every affected area, including wall cavities, subfloors and framing, reaches an acceptable drying standard. Nothing else reliably heads off secondary damage, and that damage can run thousands more to remediate, so we dry by the numbers.
