Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in St. George

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. Drywall draws the water in, the platform beneath the unit gets saturated, and moisture slides under the floor, with mold often underway before any stain appears. Our team measures how far it traveled, dries the building materials and fixes the damage. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the System DownSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Bring In Two ProsAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • A condensate pump that burned out or lost power and stopped moving water.
  • Ice on the evaporator coil melting all at once and overwhelming the pan.
  • A drain line joint that came apart or was never glued, leaking inside a wall or attic.
  • Water escaping from a drain pan that has cracked, rusted through or tilted.
  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Insulation Around the Unit

Batts or blown-in material next to the pan absorb the leak and press that moisture onto the ceiling. Once saturated it stops insulating, and replacement is common.

Nearby Flooring

From the closet, water slips under the door and works below hardwood, laminate or carpet pad. Laminate swells and will not recover, and hardwood cups if it stays wet.

Baseboards and Door Trim

The bottom of the door jamb and the baseboard beside it take on water early. Because pressed-wood trim puffs up and falls apart when wet, new material goes in.

Mold Near the Return

Within 24 to 48 hours mold can start on wet wood and drywall next to the unit, and the return may draw spores into the ducts and around the home.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

Timing is usually what decides whether an AC leak is covered. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. A drip the adjuster believes lasted weeks or months can be classed as gradual leakage or poor maintenance, and the claim denied. Repairing the air conditioner itself is typically not covered either way. Read your policy, and ask us for photos and moisture readings to support the claim.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • Mold growth, if any, and the containment and removal it calls for.
  • Whether the air handler is in the attic, a closet or a garage.
  • Whether the water is still clean or has to be handled as Category 2.
  • How much flooring, wall and ceiling material needs replacement.
