Ceiling Water Damage Repair Help in St. George

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. Long before you see discoloration, the insulation is wet, moisture has followed the framing and a puddle sits on the hidden side of the panel. It could come from an upstairs bathroom, plumbing inside the ceiling, an AC condensate line or the roof. Our team finds where it starts before any patching is done.

Painting over the stain does not fix it. If the cavity above is still wet, the mark comes back and mold can start on the paper backing within 24 to 48 hours. The opening is kept as small as the damage allows, wet insulation comes out, joists and surrounding drywall are dried, and the area is patched, textured and painted to blend. A sagging ceiling is a different matter. Stay out from under it and call.

Signs to Look For

  • Drips coming out of a ceiling light, air vent or smoke alarm
  • Stale, mildew like odor you cannot trace to anything you can see
  • Soft, bowed or sagging spots you can feel with a gentle push
  • Small black or green specks appearing in a cluster overhead
  • Yellow or brown rings that grow after rain or after someone showers
  • New fine cracks that radiate from a discolored patch
Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Older Ceiling Materials

Asbestos was used in certain older plasters and popcorn finishes. If your home has one, it should be tested before anyone scrapes, cuts or tears out a damaged area.

Wet Lights and Wiring

The damp area usually includes fan mounts, recessed fixtures and wiring boxes. Water in a fixture can short the circuit, so switch off the breaker for that room.

The Leak Is Somewhere Else

Because moisture runs along framing, plumbing and ductwork, the drip may be feet away from the entry point. Opening the ceiling only at the stain can miss wet material farther along.

A Ceiling That Lets Go

Wet drywall is far heavier than dry and loses its grip on the fasteners. An entire piece can fall from a bulging spot onto anything below.

Our Process

Our Ceiling Water Damage Repair Process

1

Find the Source

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team follows the water to its origin and inspects the attic or room overhead. The leak has to be stopped before repairs make sense.

2

Make It Safe

Furnishings are protected or relocated, and electricity to affected lights is turned off. Where water is trapped overhead, it gets drained under control into containers.

3

Open and Dry

Damaged drywall and wet insulation come out. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Rebuild and Paint

Once moisture readings are normal we install new insulation and drywall, tape and finish the seams, match the existing texture and prime with a stain blocker before painting.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Spreads Above

Moisture follows the joists and the insulation drinks it in. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

Hours 24 to 48

Stains and Bubbles

A stain forms and paint starts to blister. The paper layer becomes a place mold is able to grow, and heavy wet insulation bears down from above.

Day 3 to 7

The Ceiling Bows

The panel turns soft and starts slipping off its fasteners. Between the joists the surface dips, joints split apart and a musty smell arrives below.

After a week

Sections May Fall

Saturated pieces may drop and carry soaked insulation down too. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.
