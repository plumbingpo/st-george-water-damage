How Much Does Water Damage Cost in St. George?

Home / Blog / How Much Does Water Damage Cost in St. George?

The Short Answer

In St. George, the usual water damage bill lands in a range of $1,500 to $10,000 or more. A minor job involving clean water may cost less than roughly $1,500. Major flooding or a sewage backup that needs rebuilding can run well above $10,000.

These are typical national ranges, not a quote. Pricing in St. George, UT depends on local wages and the going rate for materials, so your number may differ. For a precise figure, there is no substitute for moisture readings taken during an on-site visit.

Cost by Water Category

Water type is normally the starting point when a restoration job is priced. The industry sorts water into three categories based on how contaminated it is. A job with dirty water calls for extra protective gear, more disinfecting and more material taken out.

Across the US, cleanup tends to run roughly $3 to $7.50 per square foot depending on category. The table below shows common rates.

CategoryWhat it isTypical cost per sq ft
Category 1 (clean water)Supply lines, faucets, water heater tanksAbout $3 to $4
Category 2, gray waterWashing machine or dishwasher discharge, sump pump failuresRoughly $4 to $6.50
Category 3 (black water)Storm surge, flooding rivers, sewageAbout $7 to $7.50

Category can change. After about two days of sitting, water that started clean carries bacteria and is reclassified to gray or black. Putting things off raises the price for this reason, among others.

What Drives the Price

An identical pipe break in two houses can lead to bills that are far apart. When a job is priced, the contractor considers the following.

  • Area affected. More wet square footage means more labor, more equipment and more days on site.
  • Water category. Clean water is the cheapest to handle. Sewage is the most expensive.
  • How long it sat. The longer water sits, the deeper it gets into walls and the subfloor. Catching a leak within an hour is much cheaper than discovering it when you return from a weekend away.
  • Materials. Tile and concrete dry well. Insulation, drywall, carpet pad and hardwood frequently have to be replaced or dried with special methods.
  • Mold. If growth has started, mold remediation is added to the scope, with containment and air filtration.
  • Reconstruction. Drying is one cost. Putting back cabinets, paint, flooring and drywall is billed separately, and for a big loss that part is frequently larger.

An estimate normally has two main parts, with water extraction coming before structural drying. In general the drying phase lasts 3 to 5 days.

A daily rate is common for dehumidifiers and air movers. If your house is dry after three days, you pay less than if it needs six.

Typical Costs for Common Jobs

For the kinds of losses that come up most frequently in St. George, national figures usually look like this. Use the numbers to get oriented and not as a firm price.

  • Burst pipe. Cleaning up and drying out often runs $1,500 to $5,000, with repair of the burst pipe billed in addition. Expect more if it ran for hours or soaked more than one floor.
  • Appliance leak. A washing machine, dishwasher or water heater leak caught early is often $1,000 to $4,000. Hidden damage makes a slow leak beneath a cabinet more expensive.
  • Basement flood. Depth, where the water came from and whether the room is finished put most jobs at $2,000 to $10,000 or more. Finished basements land near the top of the range for basement water removal.
  • Sewage backup. Commonly $3,000 to $10,000 and up. Because sewage cleanup falls under Category 3, crews disinfect the whole area and take out porous materials.
  • Roof leak. Interior work on wet ceilings, walls and insulation often comes to $1,000 to $5,000, and the roof fix is extra.

Is Water Damage in St. George Covered by Insurance?

It depends on the cause and on your policy. A typical homeowners policy often covers accidental events that happen suddenly, for example a pipe bursting or a supply line giving out with no warning.

Coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. An insurer may treat a fitting that dripped for months as a maintenance problem. Outside flooding, for instance from storm surge or a river that rises, is usually not covered without separate flood insurance. A backed-up sewer often takes an added endorsement.

Do not guess: look at your policy documents or ask the agent who sold them. Your deductible also matters. On a small job, the repair may cost less than the deductible, and a claim may not make sense.

How to Keep the Bill Down

Most of the cost of water damage is decided in the first day or two. Some basic actions go a long way.

  • Act fast. Water keeps spreading until it is removed. Each additional hour soaks more of the building.
  • Shut off the water. The moment you discover a leak, close the main shutoff, which means knowing in advance where to find it.
  • Document everything. Take photos and video before anything is moved or thrown out. Hold on to all receipts.
  • Call a pro before mold starts. Mold can begin growing in 24 to 48 hours. A job with active growth takes more days and costs more money.
  • Move what you can. Furniture, rugs and boxes should come up off wet flooring, provided that is safe.

Stay out of standing water while the electricity is on, and keep away from water that might have sewage in it. The risk of being shocked or getting sick outweighs anything you might save. When outside water is the source, flood cleanup belongs with a crew that has proper protective equipment.

Getting a Real Number

A national range helps with budgeting, yet no house is the average house. Only a moisture meter inspection reveals where the water went and what drying and repairs will be needed.

If you need water damage restoration in St. George, UT, the best time to ask for that inspection is now, while the damage is still small.
