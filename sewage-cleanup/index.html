Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. When it happens in St. George, St. George Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • In an emergency a plumber needs the cleanout, so learn where yours is and keep it in good shape
  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Schedule a camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years so trouble is found early
  • Do not wait on a slow drain, which can turn into a full blockage

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • Slow drains throughout the house that plunging does not fix

How Our Sewage Cleanup Works

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle sewage cleanup from start to finish:

1

Safety Assessment

First the crew identifies hazards, determines how much of the property the sewage has contaminated and puts containment in place to protect rooms that were not affected.

2

Sewage Removal & Material Disposal

All standing sewage is extracted. Porous materials that were contaminated, such as drywall, insulation, carpet and padding, come out and go to proper disposal as biohazard waste.

3

Cleaning and Disinfecting

EPA-registered antimicrobials are used to disinfect what is left after a thorough cleaning of each surface. Meanwhile, air scrubbers fitted with HEPA filters pull pathogens and other contaminants from the air.

4

Dry-Out and Deodorizing

Residual moisture is taken out with commercial-grade dryers. An industrial deodorizing treatment gets rid of the sewage smell. Clearance testing confirms the space is safe for occupancy.
