Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in St. George

Most of the appliance leaks we see trace back to three machines. A washing machine supply hose can split and run at full pressure until someone shuts it off. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. A refrigerator ice maker line is a thin tube that can drip behind the unit for months. Each one needs a different kind of cleanup.

Our St. George crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

Right Now

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. When either one is wet, turn the power off at the breaker.
  2. Close the Supply ValveWashers have hot and cold valves on the wall behind them. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Mop Up What You CanUse towels or a mop on whatever water is within reach, which keeps it from spreading beneath flooring and cabinets. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Leave the Door ShutA dishwasher or washer that still holds water should stay closed. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Document What FailedPhotograph the failed fitting, valve or hose, and hold on to the part itself. Your insurer and the appliance maker may both ask to see it.
The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Poor installation, such as a dishwasher set out of level or a supply fitting not fully tightened.
  • The drain hose of the washer pulling free of the standpipe, or the standpipe backing up from a clog.
  • A clogged defrost drain or cracked drain pan that lets a refrigerator leak onto the floor.
  • Overloading the drum or adding excess detergent, either of which can make the machine overflow.
  • Dishwasher parts that wear out, including the door gasket, the drain hose and the seal on the pump.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Cabinet Damage

From below, a leaking dishwasher wets the toe kicks and the side panels of the cabinets. The panels swell and separate, and wet cabinet boxes can hold moisture against the wall for weeks.

Ceiling Below an Upstairs Laundry

The ceiling under an upper floor laundry catches whatever water makes it past the floor above. Sagging, staining and soaked insulation above the drywall are the usual result.

Floors and What Is Under Them

A leak runs under laminate, hardwood or vinyl plank flooring with little resistance. Boards cup or swell, and the subfloor underneath stays wet long after the surface looks dry.

Adjoining Rooms

Water may pour from a washer hose for hours on end. It passes under walls into hallways, closets and bedrooms, and soaks carpet and pad well away from the laundry room.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

How the Quote Is Built

  • Spread of the water into other rooms, and into the ceiling beneath an upstairs laundry if there is one.

Our Process

Our Appliance Leak Water Damage Process

1

Pull and Inspect

After making sure the supply is shut and moving the disconnected appliance, our crew follows the water with meters and a thermal camera into nearby walls, under flooring and beneath cabinets.

2

Extract the Water

We extract standing water out of carpet and off hard floors. If it is trapped beneath a floating floor or the cabinets, toe kicks come off or boards are lifted so we can get to it.

3

Save Materials by Drying

We set air movers and dehumidifiers and, where possible, dry cabinets and hardwood in place with directed airflow so they do not have to be torn out.

4

Clean and Confirm

Any surface that dishwasher or washer discharge reached is treated with an antimicrobial. Before the appliance is put back, a last set of readings confirms the floor under it is no longer damp.
