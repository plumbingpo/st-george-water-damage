Dealing With Toilet Overflow Cleanup in St. George

It is tempting to grab a mop after a toilet overflows, yet the water never stays on top of the tile. The gap at the drain pipe, the wall and the space under the baseboard all take water in. What matters most is what was in it. If it came from the tank or the supply line, it is clean. Bowl water is not clean, so we handle it as contaminated from the start.

In St. George, we begin each overflow job by determining the category of water, which decides how the rest is done. With clean water, the job is extraction followed by drying. Gray or black water means protective gear, removal of soaked porous materials like carpet pad, and disinfection of every surface it touched. Then we check the subfloor, the wall bases and the room underneath with moisture meters so nothing is left wet.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stop the WaterTurn the valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. When the valve is frozen, remove the lid of the tank and hold the float up so filling stops.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainNo more flushing, and no running the washer, a shower or a sink. If the drain line is blocked, any water you send down can come back up.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterIf the water came from the bowl or contains waste, keep children and pets out, and wear gloves and waterproof shoes if you must walk through it.
  4. Check the Room BelowWith a second floor bathroom, inspect the ceiling of the room beneath. A ceiling that sags or drips means turning off that room's power and moving your things clear.
  5. Block the SpreadOld towels can stop clean water before it gets to a hallway or carpet. Leave contaminated water for a crew with proper equipment and disinfectants.
Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Waste rising out of the lowest toilet in the house because the main sewer line is blocked.
  • Waste water with nowhere to go because the septic tank is full or the drain field is saturated.
  • A child's toy, wipes or a wad of paper that gets flushed and sticks in the trap.
  • Roots growing into the sewer line, or a section that has collapsed, slowing all the drains.
  • Supply lines that give out or tanks that crack, releasing clean water across the floor.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Wet Vanity and Wall Bases

Drywall edges and the vanity base stand in the water and draw it up like a wick. Several inches of wall above floor level can hold moisture with no visible sign on the paint.

Bacteria in Building Materials

Carpet, pad, drywall and baseboards all pick up bacteria from bowl or drain water. Because disinfecting porous material is not reliable, it usually has to come out.

Subfloor Saturation

Through the grout and around the toilet base, water works its way below tile or vinyl flooring. The plywood underneath swells, delaminates and can loosen the flooring bonded to it.

Odor That Returns

An odor can return every time the weather turns humid if contaminated water dried beneath the floor or inside the bottom of a wall, and only removing the affected material ends it.

What We Do

Our Toilet Overflow Cleanup Process

1

Determine the Water Type

We find out whether the water came from the supply side or the bowl and drain, then set up the right protection and containment before any cleanup begins.

2

Extraction and Removal

Standing water is extracted first. Contaminated drywall, baseboards or carpet pad come out where necessary, and the toilet is lifted when water has reached beneath the flange and base.

3

Clean and Disinfect

Every fixture, the floor, the subfloor and the base of each wall gets cleaned and receives an antimicrobial treatment. With Category 3 water this step is done before drying equipment is switched on.

4

Dry and Verify

We set drying equipment in the bathroom and in the room underneath if water reached it. We take moisture readings until the subfloor and ceiling are back to normal levels.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

When an overflow is accidental and sudden, like a clog nobody expected or a broken fill valve, homeowners policies often cover it. Sewer or drain backup is often excluded without a backup endorsement on the policy, and an overflow from a blocked main line can fall under that exclusion. Damage from a toilet that leaked at the base for months is usually not covered. Read your policy or call your agent to find out which situation applies to you.
