Dealing With Roof Leak Water Damage in St. George

The spot on your ceiling is seldom directly below the hole in the roof. Rain slips under a lifted shingle, a cracked flashing joint or a tired skylight seal, then runs along the decking and rafters before it drops onto the insulation. A ceiling stain tends to arrive late, well after the space overhead first got wet. Our team maps everything that got wet, then dries it.

Our trade is water damage restoration, and roofing is a separate one. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. If rain is still coming in, we can tarp the roof as a temporary cover, or work alongside the roofer you choose so the leak is fixed before we close anything up.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut Power to Wet FixturesIf water is dripping through a light fixture, a ceiling fan or near an outlet, switch off that circuit at the breaker and leave the fixture alone.
  2. Catch the DripsSet buckets or pans under active drips and move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the way. Lay towels or plastic sheeting down to protect the floor.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeA ceiling that sags with trapped water can be drained: stay off to the side and make a small opening at its lowest spot, with a bucket underneath.
  4. Stay Off the RoofDo not climb onto a wet roof or crawl through a dark attic. Just observe the entry point from the room below, then hand the job to a trained crew.
  5. Photograph the DamageTake photos and video of the ceiling, the attic and any damaged belongings before you move or throw anything away, then call us to start drying.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Storm impact, such as hail or a tree branch, breaking through the shingles.
  • Aging, cracked seals around a skylight frame that no longer keep water out.
  • Flashing that has rusted, lifted or pulled away around chimneys, vents and walls.
  • Leaves and grit collecting where two roof slopes meet, damming rain until it seeps through.
  • Fasteners working loose over time, so water follows the nail shank into the attic.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Ceiling Drywall

Drywall that stays wet stains, sags and loses its strength. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.

Mold in the Attic

Mold does well in an attic that is warm, dark and full of damp wood and drywall paper. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours and spread across the underside of the decking.

Rafters and Decking

Repeated wetting makes decking, trusses and rafters turn dark, swell up and go soft. Once they rot, the frame supporting the roof is weaker.

Wall Cavities

Water that reaches the top of a wall runs down inside it, wetting insulation and the back of the drywall. Bubbling paint or a swollen baseboard may be the only sign.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

Interior damage from a sudden, accidental roof leak, like one caused by a fallen limb or wind-torn shingles, is often covered under a homeowners policy. Insurers generally turn down claims tied to wear, age or lack of upkeep, and they may handle the roof repair on different terms than the inside damage. Our team gives your adjuster photos and moisture readings, yet what is covered comes down to your policy, so check it or talk to your agent.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • The amount of ceiling and wall drywall that needs to be cut out.
  • How easy the attic is to reach, how high the ceilings are, and if the roof needs a tarp before work begins.
  • How long the roof leaked before it was found, and any mold growth.
  • Damage beyond the ceiling, such as wet floors, wall cavities or furniture.
