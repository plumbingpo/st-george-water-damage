Wet Carpet Drying in St. George

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. In St. George, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. Sewage or outside flood water means carpet and pad both come out.

What It Looks Like

  • A squishy feel under your feet, or wet socks after walking across the room
  • Ripples or loose spots where the carpet has let go of the tack strip
  • As the carpet dries, yellowish or brown stains wick to the top of the pile
  • A floor that squeaks or feels soft under the carpet where it got wet
  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it
  • A carpet corner where the backing comes apart from the face when pulled up
What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Edges

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Extract the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry Every Layer

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Wet Wall Bottoms

Under the carpet, water travels until it hits the bottom of the walls. It wicks up into the baseboards and drywall unnoticed, since the carpet hides that edge.

Clean Water Turns Dirty

A clean water spill does not remain clean for long. It collects bacteria and dirt from the pad and fibers, which can turn a simple drying job into a removal.

Mold on the Backing

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. The odor arrives a long time ahead of any visible sign on top.

Swollen Subfloor

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. Squeaks, soft spots and raised seams show up weeks after the spill is forgotten.

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Moves Outward

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Day 3 to 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

After a week

Past Saving

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.
